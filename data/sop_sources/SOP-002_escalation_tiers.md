# SOP-002: Incident Escalation Tiers

**Document ID:** SOP-002
**Owner:** Dispatch Operations
**Applies to:** All dispatchers, including night shift

## 1. Purpose
This SOP explains how to escalate a cargo incident. Every incident is either **Tier 1** (critical) or **Tier 2** (warning).

## 2. Tier 1 Escalation (Critical)
A Tier 1 incident means the cargo is likely damaged, or will be soon, if no action is taken.

**Examples of Tier 1:**
- Fresh perishables or vaccines above 8°C for more than 30 minutes, or any reading above 12°C.
- Frozen goods above 0°C.
- Reefer unit failure (see SOP-003).
- Vehicle accident or cargo theft.

**Required actions for Tier 1:**
1. Call the **Dispatch Shift Lead within 15 minutes** of detecting the incident.
2. Contact the driver and instruct them to follow the reefer failure or recovery steps.
3. Notify the **QA Manager within 1 hour**.
4. Notify the **customer account manager within 2 hours**.
5. Log the incident in the incident register with the shipment location, temperature readings, and actions taken.

## 3. Tier 2 Escalation (Warning)
A Tier 2 incident is a warning sign. The cargo is not yet damaged, but needs close monitoring.

**Examples of Tier 2:**
- Fresh perishables between 8°C and 12°C for less than 30 minutes.
- Frozen goods between -5°C and 0°C.
- Sensor not reporting for more than 2 hours.
- Delay probability above 0.7 on a perishable shipment (see SOP-004).

**Required actions for Tier 2:**
1. Contact the driver within **30 minutes** to confirm the situation.
2. Re-check the temperature after **30 minutes**.
3. If the situation gets worse, upgrade to **Tier 1**.
4. Record the warning in the shift handover notes.

## 4. Difference Between Tier 1 and Tier 2
- **Tier 1** = cargo at real risk of being lost. Escalate to the Shift Lead within 15 minutes, then QA and the customer.
- **Tier 2** = early warning. The dispatcher handles it directly, monitors, and upgrades to Tier 1 if it gets worse.
- When unsure, **treat it as Tier 1**. It is always safer to over-escalate than to lose cargo.
