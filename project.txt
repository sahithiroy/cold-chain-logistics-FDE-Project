Step 1: The business
Imagine a company called "FreshMove Logistics." It runs refrigerated trucks (reefer trucks) carrying things that spoil if they get too warm:

💉 vaccines
🥬 fresh vegetables and fruit
🐟 fish and meat
💊 medicines
Each truck has a temperature sensor and GPS. Every hour the truck sends data such as: "I'm at this location, the cargo is at 6°C, the risk is high, the delay chance is 80%."

All of this data is saved in an old company database (SQL Server), built many years ago.

Step 2: The problem
At night, a dispatcher (someone monitoring all the trucks) gets worried and wants to know:

"Are any trucks near Los Angeles getting too warm? Is the temperature against our safety rules? What should I do?"

To answer that today, the dispatcher has to:

Open the old database. The data is hard to read, with column names like IOT_TEMP_VAL_C and CGO_COND_CD, and they don't know SQL.
Check the weather separately on another website.
Search the safety rule documents (SOPs, PDF files): "What's the allowed temperature for vegetables? Who do I call?"
Put it all together mentally and make a decision.
⏱ This takes 15–30 minutes. Meanwhile, the vaccines may spoil. 💸

The problem in one line:

Important information is scattered across an old database, weather websites, and PDF documents, so people can't make fast decisions when cargo is at risk.

Step 3: What we're building
We're building an AI assistant (chatbot) for the dispatcher, like ChatGPT but connected to the company's own data.

The dispatcher simply types:

"Any trucks near Los Angeles too warm? Does it break our rules?"

and the AI does all the work by itself:


Dispatcher asks a question
        │
        ▼
   🤖 AI Assistant (the "brain")
        │
        ├──► 1. Looks in the truck database   → "Truck #12 near LA is at 9°C"
        ├──► 2. Checks the weather            → "LA is 35°C today, heatwave"
        ├──► 3. Reads the safety rules (SOP)  → "Vegetables must stay 2–8°C.
        │                                        Above 8°C = Tier 1 emergency"
        ▼
   Answer in about 5 seconds:
   "⚠️ Truck #12 is at 9°C, above the 8°C limit. There's a heatwave in LA.
    This is a Tier 1 breach. Inform your team lead within 15 minutes."
15–30 minutes → about 5 seconds. That's the value.

Step 4: What each phase is (simply)
Phase	Simple meaning	Real-life comparison
0. Load data	Put the truck data into the old database	Setting up the company's old filing cabinet
1. Safety rules (RAG)	Teach the AI to read and search the safety documents	Giving the assistant the rulebook
2. Security	Let the AI only read a clean copy of the data, never change or delete it	Giving an intern a photocopy, not the original files
3. AI agent	Build the brain that decides "should I check the database? the weather? the rules?"	The assistant who knows which drawer to open
4. Audit log	Record everything the AI did and why	A CCTV camera so managers can check later
5. Website (UI)	A simple screen with chat, map, and alerts	The dashboard the dispatcher uses
6. Deploy	Put it on a cloud server (AWS) so anyone in the company can use it	Opening the shop to customers
Step 5: Why it's called an "FDE" project
An AI FDE is the person a company sends to a customer to say:

"Your data is messy and old? No problem. I'll connect AI to it safely and solve your real problem."

This project practices exactly that:

✅ dealing with old, messy systems (the legacy database)
✅ keeping it safe (read-only access, audit logs)
✅ using AI to solve a real business problem (spoiled cargo, slow decisions)
✅ delivering something people can actually use (a website on the cloud)
Summary
Problem: Trucks carry cargo that spoils when warm. The information needed to act is scattered in an old database, weather sites, and rule documents, so decisions are slow.

Solution: An AI assistant that pulls all of it together and tells the dispatcher what's wrong and what to do, in seconds.
